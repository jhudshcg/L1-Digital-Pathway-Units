# Word Processing Software — v2 delivery plan

Unit D/505/6398. Read alongside the assessment criteria in spec.md, which are unchanged.

## Audience and approach
UK Level 1 learners, 16+, pre-GCSE, with varied learning needs. Short demonstration, small practical task, quick check, then independent project work. Word is the first unit. No Git or coding is needed by students for this unit; resources remain in the student submodule.

Use the three existing Word examples as editable template samples, not completed assessed projects. Learners try changes in copies, then choose a Word template or blank document for their own guide. Remove sample text and unused features.

## Core project
Create a five-page guide about a personal interest: cover, automatic contents, and three short content pages. Short paragraphs, lists, images and tables can share the pages; five pages does not mean five pages of continuous writing.

Use Title and Subtitle on the cover, Heading 1 for sections, and Heading 2 for a subsection. Include readable body text (12pt or larger to meet reader needs), a bullet or numbered list, one table that the learner creates and modifies, two images, one text box, one meaningful hyperlink, suitable A4 page layout and page numbers. Modify one existing style. Check and correct the work. Save and reopen the DOCX and export/check a PDF.

Heading 3, creating new styles, section breaks, running headers and Page X of Y are extensions. Core features are project requirements; the qualification criteria remain the assessment standard. Judge demonstrated skills, not decorative complexity or quantity of prose. Plan individual support without removing assessment criteria.

## Booklet tasks/activities with assessment criteria mapping
0. First steps (practice): type and edit a short paragraph, apply a heading, save, close and reopen a practice document. Diagnostic check, no separate written reflection.
1. Explore templates and choose a topic (1.1, 1.2, 1.5): inspect the three samples, edit a copy of one, compare available template uses, then choose a template or blank start. Complete only learner details and section 1 of the existing planning form now. Complete sourcing, structure and styling sections later as the corresponding skills are learned. Save the form itself; do not duplicate it as a screenshot. Evidence: short topic/information note and two template-use entries; completed form.
2. Gather and combine information (1.3, 1.4): type own short text and insert two images and a link from at least two sources overall. Record source and permission/credit information. Evidence: source log, content screenshot snippet in Task 2 and actual guide; teacher observation of input if needed.
3. Structure the guide (2.2, 3.1): apply Heading 1 and Heading 2, modify one heading style, insert and update automatic contents. Evidence: heading/contents screenshot snippets, actual guide and one short reason for a style choice.
4. Format text (3.1, 3.2): adjust readable paragraph spacing through Normal style, add list and text box. Evidence: list/text-box screenshot snippets, actual guide and one short formatting choice.
5. Create and change a table (2.1): create a small table, then add a row or column and adjust width/style. Evidence: table screenshot snippet, actual table and short change record or teacher observation.
6. Set up pages (3.3): A4, suitable margins/orientation, page breaks and simple page numbers; check print preview. Evidence: page-layout screenshot snippet, actual document and short preview check.
7. Review and correct (1.6, 3.4): use proofing/editing tools and peer or teacher feedback; make and record two useful corrections. Evidence: before/after notes and tool used; checked document. Additional corrections as needed.
8. Save, retrieve and export (1.7): save to own college OneDrive folder, reopen using that folder, make a further edit and save; export/reopen PDF. Evidence: folder/name and dated retrieval observation or student demonstration.
9. Final check (3.1, 3.4): check core features and reader needs, make remaining corrections, submit guide DOCX/PDF, form and booklet. Evidence: checklist and brief reflection.

Assessor grid records each individual criterion, evidence location and further action. Authenticity declaration ends the booklet. Use focused screenshot snippets from the project guide in Booklet Tasks 2–6, alongside short notes and the completed planning form. Keep the editable guide available to verify styles, automatic contents and other features that screenshots cannot prove. A printed guide may be handed in as additional evidence beside the booklet. Teacher observations supplement evidence of actions.

## Suggested pace (3 x 90 minutes each week)
Week 1: first steps, sample exploration, topic and first short content section.
Week 2: sources and combining text/images; continue content.
Week 3: headings, contents, paragraph style and lists.
Week 4: table, text box and page layout.
Week 5: finish content, peer review and corrections.
Week 6: retrieval, PDF check and submission.
Week 7: targeted support and resubmission; weeks 8–9: buffer and extensions.
Use a short demo and check each lesson, with most time for practice/project work. Adjust pace after the first-lesson diagnostic.
