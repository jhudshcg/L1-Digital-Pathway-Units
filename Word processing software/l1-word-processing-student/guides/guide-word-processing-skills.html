1. Setting Up Your 5+ Page Document & Choosing Templates

In this project, you will build an engaging, structured 5+ page guide on a topic you enjoy (such as your favourite video game, sport, hobby, film series, automotive topic, or music artist).

Why Plan Your Document Structure First?

Starting with a clear page plan prevents you from getting stuck halfway through. It ensures your document looks like a real magazine, book, or professional report rather than a messy wall of random text.

When to Use Templates vs. Blank Documents

  • Word Templates: Pre-made documents that already have built-in color themes, fonts, and layout boxes (e.g., resumes, event flyers, brochures). Use templates when you need a fast, standardized design.
  • Blank Documents with Custom Styles: Starting from a blank page gives you total creative freedom to choose your exact heading hierarchy, page structure, and custom colors.
📋 Your 5-Page Minimum Structure:
  • Page 1 — Cover Page: Eye-catching Title, Subtitle, Featured Graphic/Photo, Learner Name, and Date.
  • Page 2 — Table of Contents & Introduction: Automated ToC, welcoming intro paragraph, and guide overview.
  • Page 3 — Section 1 (Core Knowledge): Background history, key rules, main characters, or how-to steps.
  • Page 4 — Section 2 (Data & Tips): Comparison table with stats/specs, plus a styled Callout Quote box.
  • Page 5 — Section 3 (Pro Recommendations & Summary): Advanced advice, curated web links, and final summary.

2. Heading Styles vs Manual Formatting (Why Styles Win)

A common beginner mistake is highlighting text and manually clicking Bold and 18pt font to make headings. In professional document design, always use Word's built-in Styles Gallery on the Home ribbon tab.

Why Are Styles So Much Better Than Manual Formatting?
  • ⚡ 1-Click Document Restyling: If your teacher or client asks to change font from Blue Arial to Navy Calibri across 20 pages, you change the Style once and all 50 headings update instantly. With manual formatting, you'd have to edit every single heading by hand!
  • 📑 Powers the Table of Contents: Word cannot generate an automatic Table of Contents unless you use Heading 1, Heading 2, etc.
  • 🧭 Instant Navigation Pane: Press Ctrl + F to see an interactive outline of your document and jump between sections in a second.
  • ♿ Accessibility: Screen readers used by visually impaired readers use heading tags to read aloud the structure of the document.

Manual Formatting (Slow & Fragile)

Manually selecting text and clicking font size, color, and bold on each line. Inconsistent sizes, breaks the Table of Contents, and takes ages to fix.

Word Styles (Fast & Professional)

Clicking Heading 1 or Heading 2 in the Styles bar. Keeps every heading perfectly uniform, enables 1-click ToC, and saves hours.

Level Word Style Name How & Where to Use It
Document Title Title Page 1 main title (e.g. The Ultimate Elden Ring Beginner's Guide)
Subtitle Subtitle Page 1 tagline (e.g. Essential Combat Strategies, Map Secrets & Character Builds)
Main Heading Heading 1 Top of every major section (e.g. 1. Choosing Your Starting Class)
Sub-Heading Heading 2 Sub-topics within a section (e.g. Vagabond vs. Astrologer)
Body Text Normal Regular explanation paragraphs (12pt clean font)

3. Generating an Automated Table of Contents (ToC)

Never type a Table of Contents manually with full stops and page numbers! Word creates and updates it for you in seconds.

Why Use an Automated Table of Contents?

When you type dots (Section 1 ......... Page 3) manually, adding just one image or paragraph pushes text down, making all your page numbers wrong. Word's Automatic Table of Contents scans your document headings and updates all page numbers in a single click.

Step-by-Step: How to Insert Your Table of Contents

  1. Click where you want the table to appear (usually on Page 2).
  2. Open the References tab on the Word ribbon.
  3. Click the Table of Contents button on the far left.
  4. Select Automatic Table 1 or Automatic Table 2.
References Tab ➔ Table of Contents ➔ Automatic Table 1
🔄 How to Update Your Table of Contents When Pages Change:

If you add new sections, change headings, or add pictures that push text onto another page:

  1. Click on your Table of Contents box.
  2. Click the Update Table button at the top of the box.
  3. Choose Update entire table ➔ click OK. Done!

4. Formatting Body Text, Lists & Callout Quote Boxes

Why Paragraph Spacing & Callout Boxes Matter

Large blocks of cramped text cause eye fatigue. Proper line spacing gives text breathing room, while styled Callout Boxes draw the reader's eye immediately to key tips, warnings, or quotes.

Standard Body Text Guidelines

  • Font Size: Set body paragraphs to 12pt (e.g., Calibri, Aptos, or Arial).
  • Line Spacing: Set to 1.15 line spacing.
  • Paragraph Spacing: Set 6pt Space After paragraphs so you never have to spam the Enter key twice between paragraphs.
  • Lists: Use Bullet Points for unordered items and Numbered Lists for step-by-step instructions.

How to Insert a Styled Callout Text Box

  1. Go to the Insert tab ➔ click Text Box ➔ choose Simple Text Box.
  2. Type a key tip, definition, or pro advice quote inside.
  3. Click the box outline to open the Shape Format ribbon:
    • Shape Fill: Pick a soft background color (e.g. Light Blue or Soft Gray).
    • Shape Outline: Choose a clean 1.5pt solid border.
Insert ➔ Text Box ➔ Simple Text Box ➔ Shape Format

5. Inserting & Styling Data Tables

Why Use Tables Instead of Spacebar or Tabs?

Trying to line up columns by pressing the Spacebar or Tab key always creates jagged, misaligned columns when printed or opened on different screens. Tables lock information into neat, readable rows and columns with crisp borders.

Step-by-Step Table Creation:

  1. Go to Insert ➔ click Table ➔ drag the grid to choose your size (e.g., 4 columns × 5 rows).
  2. Header Row Shading: Select the top row ➔ go to Table Design tab ➔ click Shading ➔ choose a dark color and set text to white bold.
  3. Add/Remove Rows or Columns: Right-click any cell ➔ choose Insert ➔ Insert Rows Below or Insert Columns Right.
  4. Ensure Borders are Visible: Select the whole table ➔ go to Table Design ➔ Borders ➔ choose All Borders.
Insert ➔ Table ➔ Table Design ➔ Shading & Borders

6. Working with Images & Text Wrapping

Why Text Wrapping is Essential

By default, Word treats inserted pictures like giant letters of text ("In Line with Text"), leaving awkward giant blank gaps. Changing text wrapping to Square or Tight lets paragraphs flow neatly around your image, giving a magazine-quality layout.

Inserting & Formatting an Image:

  1. Go to Insert ➔ Pictures ➔ choose This Device... (or stock images).
  2. Click the picture to reveal the Picture Format tab on the ribbon.
  3. Click Wrap Text ➔ select Square or Top and Bottom.
  4. Resize the image using the corner handles (hold Shift if needed to maintain aspect ratio without stretching).
  5. Add a subtle border or picture style for a polished look.
Insert ➔ Pictures ➔ Picture Format ➔ Wrap Text (Square)

7. Adding Meaningful Web Hyperlinks

Why Use Meaningful Link Text?

Pasting a raw URL like https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Topic?id=98374829374 looks messy and clutters your page. Turning natural words (e.g. "Read the Official Game Wiki") into clickable links looks clean, professional, and accessible.

How to Insert a Hyperlink:

  1. Highlight the specific text you want to become clickable (e.g. Official Tournament Rules).
  2. Press the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + K (or go to Insert ➔ Link).
  3. In the Address box at the bottom, paste the web URL (e.g. https://www.ea.com).
  4. Click OK. The text will turn blue and underlined, ready for readers to Ctrl+Click.

8. Page Setup, Section Breaks, Running Headers & Page Numbers

Why Section Breaks Beat Hitting Enter 20 Times

If you press Enter over and over to push text onto the next page, adding even a single line of text later pushes everything on future pages completely out of position. Page Breaks and Section Breaks cleanly separate pages and allow you to have a cover page without page numbers while your body pages have headers and footers.

Setting Up Margins & Breaks

  • Margins: Go to Layout ➔ Margins ➔ select Normal (2.54 cm / 1 inch).
  • Page Break: Press Ctrl + Enter to immediately jump to a clean new page.
  • Section Break (Next Page): Go to Layout ➔ Breaks ➔ Next Page. Use this after your Cover Page.

Adding Running Headers & "Page X of Y" Footers

  1. Double-click the top margin of Page 2 to open the Header & Footer ribbon.
  2. Tick the box for Different First Page (this keeps your cover page free of clutter).
  3. Type your guide's title in the Header (e.g. Rocket League Player's Handbook).
  4. Move to the Footer: Click Page Number ➔ Bottom of Page ➔ choose Page X of Y (e.g. Page 2 of 6).
  5. Double-click the middle of the page to exit Header & Footer editing.
Insert ➔ Header & Footer ➔ Different First Page ➔ Page Number (Page X of Y)

9. Proofing Tools, Spell Check & Peer Review

Why Check Before You Submit?

Typos, repeated words, and broken formatting ruin an otherwise great document. Proofing tools catch errors automatically, and peer review gives you honest feedback before your final assessment.

Top Proofing Tools to Use

  • Spelling & Grammar (Editor): Press F7 or click Review ➔ Editor to scan your entire document for spelling mistakes and grammar improvements.
  • Find & Replace: Press Ctrl + H to instantly find and fix words that you may have misspelled repeatedly.
  • Thesaurus: Highlight a word and press Shift + F7 to find more exciting, accurate vocabulary.
👥 Peer Learning Activity: Swap documents with a partner in class. Go to the Review tab ➔ click New Comment to leave 3 helpful suggestions on their document, and record the improvements in your booklet log.

10. Saving Files & Exporting as PDF

Why Export to PDF?

A .docx file might shift its layout or replace fonts if opened on another computer or phone. A PDF (Portable Document Format) freezes your layout, photos, and fonts permanently so it looks identical on any phone, tablet, PC, or printer.

Saving & Exporting Steps:

  1. Save Word Document: Go to File ➔ Save As ➔ name your file clearly using standard naming conventions (e.g., AlexSmith-GamingGuide.docx).
  2. Export as PDF: Go to File ➔ Export ➔ click Create PDF/XPS Document ➔ click Publish.
