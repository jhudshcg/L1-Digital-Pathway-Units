📋 1. Researching Your Canteen Menu

Before designing your ordering app, you need to research and choose a balanced, appealing menu for your college canteen. You can record your research in an Excel spreadsheet or directly in your Project Booklet table.

Minimum Menu Requirements Checklist:
  • 🥗 At least 4 Mains (e.g. chicken burger, veggie wrap, pizza slice, baked potato, chilli bowl)
  • 🍪 At least 3 Snacks / Desserts (e.g. crisps, chocolate brownie, fresh fruit salad pot, flapjack)
  • 🥤 At least 3 Drinks (e.g. orange sparkling water, chocolate milkshake, iced tea, hot coffee)
  • ⭐ At least 3 Healthy Items (highlight items with low sugar, fresh fruit/veg, or high fibre)
  • ⚠️ Allergy Information for each item (identify common allergens: gluten, dairy, nuts, eggs, fish, soy)

📸 2. Understanding Image Copyright & Saving Files

When creating digital designs, you must respect intellectual property and copyright laws. You cannot simply copy random photos from Google Images without permission.

Safe Royalty-Free Image Libraries:
  • Unsplash (unsplash.com) — High-quality free stock photos.
  • Pexels (pexels.com) — Free food photography library.
  • Pixabay (pixabay.com) — Free food graphics and icons.

File Organisation Rule: Download your 10 images directly into your project's images/ folder (e.g. images/pizza.jpg). Do not use external web links that can expire or break when offline!

🎨 3. Creating Your Mood Board

A mood board is a visual collage that establishes the creative direction, colour palette, typography, and mood of your canteen app before you begin styling.

How to Build Your Mood Board:
  1. Pick a theme concept (e.g. 50s American Diner, Neon Street Food Punk, Fresh Green Garden Cafe, or Retro College Tuckshop).
  2. Select 3–4 theme colours (e.g. Primary Red, Warm Cream, Charcoal, Mustard).
  3. Collect 4–6 inspiration photos of food styling, menus, neon signs, or logos.
  4. Arrange them on a digital canvas using provector.app or a graphics drawing tool.
  5. Export your mood board (as PNG or JPG) and paste it into Task 7 of your Project Booklet.

📐 4. Wireframing Your User Interface (UI)

A wireframe is a simplified visual blueprint showing where buttons, titles, cards, and summaries sit on the screen. It helps you organize your interface before touching code.

What a Wireframe IS and IS NOT:
  • It DOES include: Rectangular element outlines, button shapes, labels, text placeholders, and crossed boxes [ ✕ ] to represent where pictures will go.
  • It DOES NOT include: Finished colour schemes, real photos, detailed illustrations, or decorated fonts. Keep it simple and focused purely on page layout!
